01 — Theoretical background

Why is there a ΔT between the winding and the sensor?

Heat generated in the stator windings must travel a physical path to reach the temperature sensor. Each material along that path (copper, insulating varnish, laminated core, encapsulation resin) presents a resistance to thermal conduction. This resistance, combined with the dissipated power, creates a permanent temperature difference during operation.

The RC thermal model is the simplest and most effective representation for quantifying this phenomenon: thermal resistance (Rth) determines the steady-state gradient, and thermal capacitance (Cth) determines how quickly the system responds to load changes.

Thermal resistance (Rth)

Analogous to electrical resistance. Determines the steady-state ΔT for a given dissipated power. Unit: °C/W. The higher the Rth between winding and sensor, the larger the undetected gradient.

Thermal capacitance (Cth)

Analogous to a capacitor. Represents the thermal mass of the system — the larger it is, the slower the response. Determines the time constant τ = Rth × Cth, which defines how many minutes the system takes to stabilise.

Loss power (P_loss)

Fraction of electrical power converted to heat through Joule losses in the windings, iron losses (hysteresis + eddy currents) and friction. Estimated as: P_electrical × (1 − η).

Time constant (τ)

Extracted directly from telemetry by the temperature rise rate observed in the chart. Between 15:12 and 15:22, temperature rose ~95°C in ~10 min, indicating τ ≈ 14 min for this motor.

Main equation — steady state
T_winding = T_sensor_read + (P_loss × Rth_winding→sensor)
where:
P_perda = P_elétrica × (1 − η)    [W]
Rth = thermal resistance between winding and sensor    [°C/W]
η = motor efficiency    [dimensionless, 0–1]
Transient response — temperature as a function of time
T(t) = T_amb + (P_perda × Rth) × (1 − e−t/τ)
τ = Rth × Cth    (time constant, estimated at ~14 min from telemetry)

05 — Analysis conclusions

Findings and recommendations

FAILURE 01

Mispositioned sensor

Sensor in peripheral resin (2–3 o'clock) creates significant thermal lag. The resin's thermal resistance prevents accurate reading of actual winding temperature, generating an estimated ΔT of 10–30°C depending on the actual Rth.

FAILURE 02

Controller protection did not activate

Even with the sensor reading close to the 130°C threshold, the controller did not trigger derating or shutdown. This is independent of sensor positioning — it indicates an additional firmware or configuration issue.

FINDING 01

Prolonged exposure above 130°C

Motor temperature above threshold for more than 2 continuous hours. Class B insulation varnish (130°C) irreversibly collapses under these conditions, regardless of prior thermal cycle count.

FINDING 02

Localised burn — pre-existing asymmetry

O padrão de queima concentrado (10h–2h) sugere que havia um ponto de lowest dielectric strength antes da falha — seja por umidade localizada, vibração crônica, ou defeito de fabricação no isolamento.

⚠ Correcting only the sensor position is insufficient. The controller's failure to activate protection must be investigated separately before returning vehicles with this configuration to operation.
